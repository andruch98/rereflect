# Ask Genius — Survey Answer Examples

## Introduction

This file contains five worked examples for answering managers’ questions about resident surveys. **These examples apply to surveys only. They do not apply to public reviews or saved review analyses.** For a question about both sources, apply this guidance only to the survey portion and keep survey and review findings distinct. For a reviews-only question, use the review instructions and review evidence.

All five examples are provided together. Read each manager’s question with its conversation context and supporting data, then compare that evidence with the original response and its assessment:

- **Good:** The original response demonstrates the desired behavior. “Suggested response: None” means no correction is needed; it does not mean the manager should receive no answer.
- **Incorrect or incomplete:** The original response demonstrates a mistake or omission. Do not imitate that response. Use “Why this assessment” to understand the problem and “Suggested response” to see the preferred answer.
- **Lesson:** Apply the principle to the current question and evidence. Adapt the wording and level of detail rather than copying a stock answer. The shared appendix preserves the complete original questions and answers behind these examples.

The examples and appendix contain fictional test submissions, including their counts, property name, ratings and comments. They are demonstrations, not evidence for the current request. Base the current answer and any source references only on the authorized records supplied with that request. Do not fill gaps in current evidence with facts from these examples.

These examples illustrate answer content. Continue to follow the current instructions for response format and source references. Return the answer to the manager’s question; do not reproduce this file’s assessment fields.

## Example 1 — Survey counts by type

**Manager’s question:** Break it down by survey type.

**Conversation context:** The manager had just asked how many completed survey responses were available. The answer was nine. The period was All available history and the source was surveys.

**Supporting data:** Calculated survey counts: move-out 1; lease renewed 1; 9-month renewal planning 1; 6-month 1; 3-month 1; maintenance 3; move-in 1. Total: 9 completed submissions.

**Original response — good:**

> - Move-out completed: 1 response
> - Lease renewed: 1 response
> - 9-month renewal planning: 1 response
> - 6-month: 1 response
> - 3-month: 1 response
> - Maintenance completed: 3 responses
> - Move-in completed: 1 response

**Why this assessment:**

- Includes all seven questionnaire types and the correct count for each; the counts add to nine.
- Counts separate maintenance submissions rather than collapsing them into one response.
- Keeps the established survey source and period. It does not invent explanations or sentiment from counts.

**Suggested response:** None.

**Lesson:** Use the supplied counts and preserve the counted unit and established scope.

## Example 2 — Identify the issue in a follow-up

**Manager’s question:** What issue is it?

**Conversation context:** The manager was discussing maintenance, then asked what the bad response said. That response had overall service 2 — Bad, issue unresolved, and a comment about continued leaking and a near-fall. “What issue is it?” refers to that response. The available survey data contains the three maintenance submissions.

**Supporting data:** The 2 — Bad maintenance submission describes the maintenance request as “a fictional leaking kitchen faucet”; issue resolution “No, still unresolved”; response time “Not sure”; original comment “it is still leaaking a lot and i nearly fell with the watr”.

**Original response — good:**

> The issue in the bad maintenance survey is a leaking kitchen faucet, as described in the survey context and request description.

**Why this assessment:**

- Identifies the kitchen faucet from the original request context instead of guessing from the word “water”.
- Keeps the subject of the preceding exchange and answers the narrow question directly.
- Does not invent an injury, a response delay, a different maintenance problem or a completed repair.

**Suggested response:** None.

**Lesson:** Use history to resolve what the manager means, then use current source data to establish the fact.

## Example 3 — Identify surveys needing attention

**Manager’s question:** Which are the bad surveys? Summarize them in a list.

**Conversation context:** The manager had discussed several individual questionnaire types, then broadened the question to all surveys in All available history. All nine submissions were supplied.

**Supporting data:** Move-in: overall 3, apartment condition 2, unresolved No, comment “all good”. Maintenance: one 2/unresolved/near-fall, one 3/partially resolved/still dripping, one 4/fully resolved. The 3-month response rates living 5 and help 4, but explicitly requests follow-up and says “rent payments”.

**Original response — incorrect and incomplete:**

> The bad surveys based on lower ratings or unresolved issues are:
>
> - Move-in completed: Apartment condition rated 2 — Bad, overall move-in experience 3 — Okay.
> - Maintenance completed: One bad overall maintenance rating (2 — Bad) with unresolved issue; one partially resolved with okay maintenance rating; one good maintenance rating (4 — Good).
>
> These surveys reveal concerns primarily about apartment condition at move-in and maintenance issues with resolution delays.

**Why this assessment:**

- Correctly identifies poor apartment condition and two maintenance concerns.
- Incorrectly includes the good, fully resolved maintenance response in a list introduced as lower-rated or unresolved responses.
- Claims “resolution delays”, which the supplied answers do not establish. “Not sure” is not a negative response-time score.
- Under its own stated criterion of low ratings OR unresolved issues, it also needs the 3-month response with an explicit unresolved follow-up request. The high overall rating does not cancel that answer.
- The near-fall is material context for the unresolved leak and should be stated. Keep “bad ratings” distinct from otherwise positive responses needing follow-up.

**Suggested response:**

> Using low individual ratings or explicit unresolved concerns as the criteria, four responses need attention:
>
> - **Maintenance:** 2 — Bad; leak unresolved. The resident reports nearly falling because of the water.
> - **Maintenance:** 3 — Okay; partly resolved, with continued dripping.
> - **Move-in:** Apartment condition 2 — Bad; overall experience 3 — Okay. The resident selected no unresolved issue and wrote “all good”.
> - **3-month:** An unresolved concern needs follow-up; the comment is “rent payments”, despite positive overall ratings.
>
> The maintenance response rated 4 — Good with the issue fully resolved does not meet these criteria.

**Lesson:** Define the inclusion criterion, apply it to every supplied response, and do not invent a cause or delay.

## Example 4 — Summarize improvements across surveys

**Manager’s question:** What are the main improvements suggested by all surveys?

**Conversation context:** The manager asks for an overview in a new conversation. All nine completed survey submissions and their 36 answers are available for All available history.

**Supporting data:** Apartment condition at move-in: 2 — Bad, despite “all good” and no unresolved move-in issue. Two maintenance issues are unresolved or partially resolved; the unresolved respondent reported a near-fall. The 6-month response selects Parking and says “PARKING TOO hrd at night”. The 3-month response explicitly requests follow-up and says “rent payments”.

**Original response — incomplete:**

> Main suggested improvements from surveys:
>
> - **Parking:** Mentioned as the top area needing improvement in the 6-month survey, described as "PARKING TOO hrd at night".
> - **Rent payments:** Raised as an unresolved concern in the 3-month survey submission comment.
> - **Maintenance:** Mixed feedback in maintenance surveys, including ongoing issues with a leaking kitchen faucet, with comments indicating partial or no resolution and safety concerns from leaks.

**Why this assessment:**

- Correctly includes parking, rent-payment follow-up and unresolved/partly resolved maintenance.
- Mentions safety generally but omits the specific reported near-fall.
- Omits the 2 — Bad apartment-condition rating. This is an implied improvement signal from a rating, not a written suggestion, and should be labeled that way.

**Suggested response:**

> Across these responses, improvement signals are:
>
> - **Maintenance and safety:** One leak remains unresolved and another partly resolved. One resident reports nearly falling because of the water.
> - **Move-in condition:** Apartment condition received 2 — Bad. This is a signal from the rating; the resident also selected no unresolved issue and wrote “all good”.
> - **Parking:** The 6-month response selects Parking and says “PARKING TOO hrd at night”.
> - **Rent-payment follow-up:** The 3-month response requests help with an unresolved concern and says “rent payments”; the specific problem is not explained.

**Lesson:** Summarize structured ratings and written comments together; keep important qualifications and distinguish explicit suggestions from inferred improvement signals.

## Example 5 — Assess what surveys say about rent value

**Manager’s question:** Do you think we charge good rent?

**Conversation context:** The manager asks whether the rent is good, using the available survey feedback. All nine submissions are in scope; no market-pricing data is supplied.

**Supporting data:** 9-month planning: overall living 5 — Excellent, renewal intent Undecided, main future decision factor Rent and fees, no comment. 3-month: follow-up requested Yes and comment “rent payments”. Lease renewal: overall and process 4 — Good, reason Few suitable alternatives, no comment. No survey directly asks for or supplies a rent-value rating.

**Original response — incorrect:**

> There is no direct survey feedback about whether rent is considered good or fair. However, "Rent and fees" was highlighted as the main factor influencing renewal decisions at 9 months and was also the primary concern affecting the renewal intent, suggesting some resident attention to rental costs. No explicit positive or negative rating of rent value was provided.

**Why this assessment:**

- Correctly says the data does not directly establish whether rent is good or fair.
- Then overstates “Rent and fees” as a primary concern affecting renewal intent. The original answer identifies a decision factor, not dissatisfaction or the cause of being undecided.
- The comment “rent payments” does not establish unaffordability, incorrect charges, late payments or a payment-system problem.
- Renewing because of few suitable alternatives is not evidence that the rent offers good value.

**Suggested response:**

> We cannot tell whether rent is good value from these responses.
>
> - **9-month planning:** The resident is undecided about renewal and selects “Rent and fees” as the factor that will matter most. That does not establish that rent is too high or that it caused the uncertainty.
> - **3-month:** The resident requests follow-up and writes “rent payments”, without explaining the problem.
> - **Lease renewal:** The reason given is “Few suitable alternatives”, which does not demonstrate good rent value.
>
> No response directly rates rent value.

**Lesson:** State what the evidence supports and what it cannot establish; do not turn a selected factor into an invented complaint or cause.
